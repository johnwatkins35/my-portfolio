Unlock assistant for the campus help desk

A job description, a value plan, and a governance note for an assistant that drafts account-unlock work on a fictional campus desk. The person on the desk still performs the unlock. This is a writing sample, tied to the SysDesk lab. It is not a production system and it is not from a government network.

The work it supports

Lockouts and expired passwords are the repeatable tickets. The risky ones look the same at first glance: a disabled contractor, two people with the same name at different clients, or a lockout that is an attack rather than a mistyped password. The assistant keeps the ordinary path short and stops at the edge of those cases.

Agent job description

Name
Campus unlock assistant
Purpose
Turn a lockout or password-reset ticket into a checked draft the analyst can finish.
May do
Read the ticket. Check that the note will include the person’s name, sign-in name, computer, and client. Draft the unlock or reset steps from the desk standard. Draft the ticket note.
Must hand back
The unlock or the password reset itself. A disabled account. A name that exists at more than one client. A lockout that does not look like a bad password. Anything that needs a domain administrator.
Done
The analyst has confirmed the right account, taken the action, and saved a note that names the account, the client, the action, and whether the lockout came from a bad password.

What the analyst still does

  • Confirm identity before any unlock. Name, sign-in name, computer, and client all have to match the ticket.
  • Unlock the account, or reset the password and require a change at next sign-in. Read a temporary password on the call. Do not email it.
  • Leave a disabled contractor locked until the staffing office says the person is back.
  • When security asks, paste the lockout event into the ticket before the unlock.
  • Escalate domain-controller problems, security incidents, and work that needs a domain administrator.

Value plan

I would track four numbers on this desk. I am not claiming these results from a live queue.

  • Minutes from claim to resolve on lockout and password-reset tickets.
  • Wrong-account unlocks. The target is zero.
  • Share of lockout tickets that include the lockout event when security asked for it.
  • Share of those tickets where the analyst started from the assistant’s draft. That is the adoption number.

Governance

  • The ticket text is internal. The assistant does not need, and must not store, the new password.
  • A person remains the owner of the unlock. The assistant does not get an account that can change passwords.
  • Turn it off if wrong-account unlocks show up, or if analysts stop checking identity and file the draft as the note.
  • Review it when the desk standard changes, and retire it if the four numbers stop moving.
