Harborline, in numbers

Harborline is a fictional regional software and support company. This note is the 2026 book on the Harborline dashboard: won revenue for January through October, compared with the same months in 2025. The workbook is harborline-dashboard.xlsx.

What the dashboard is for

Four questions sit on one sheet. How big is the book? Is it growing? Where does the money sit? Is the win rate healthy? Year, quarter, region, product line, and status filter the scorecards, the three charts, and the detail table together. Growth uses the same filters on both years. The Year slicer chooses which year is on the charts.

The 2026 book, before any filter

Revenue
$19.7 million, up 7.2 percent. The dollar gain is $1.3 million, from $18.4 million in 2025.
Active seats
19,496, up 15.4 percent from 16,889.
Conversion
80.2 percent, down 4.3 points from 84.5 percent.
YoY growth
7.2 percent, the same revenue change, shown as a rate and as +$1,315,941.

Revenue is won quotes only. A lost quote and an open quote stay in the table at $0. Seats are counted on won deals. Conversion is won deals divided by closed deals. An open quote is neither a win nor a loss. The book is larger, seats grew faster than dollars, and the win rate got worse.

Where the growth comes from

  • Cloud Seats are $8.3 million, up 19.5 percent. That line added $1.35 million. The company added $1.32 million, because Hardware Refresh lost $768 thousand in the same period.
  • Security Add-ons are $1.9 million, up 43.2 percent, a gain of $564 thousand. Conversion on that line is 85.7 percent, against 80.2 percent for the company.
  • Hardware Refresh is $3.5 million, down 18.0 percent. Conversion there is 73.2 percent.
  • Support Plans stay near $500 thousand a month. The line does not drive the growth.
  • Training is seasonal. February is $79 thousand, March is $209 thousand, and September is $190 thousand.

Two weak spots

Mountain won $2.4 million, up 1.2 percent, or $28 thousand. The company grew 7.2 percent. On the stacked chart, Mountain’s bar carries a much larger Hardware Refresh share than West, Northeast, Central, or South. Hardware is the declining line, so the region lags.

South won $3.3 million, up 7.0 percent, or $219 thousand. Conversion there is 66.7 percent, down 17.8 points from 84.5 percent in 2025. The book still grows. The win rate is the problem.

Pacific South is the top territory, at $2.9 million. West is the largest region, and most of that bar is Cloud Seats.

What a slicer changes

  • Q1 2026 is $5.8 million, up 6.6 percent from Q1 2025. Q3 is $6.0 million, up 7.4 percent. The line chart keeps only the months in the selected quarter.
  • Mountain alone replaces the 7.2 percent company chip with 1.2 percent.
  • Security Add-ons alone replaces it with 43.2 percent.
  • Status set to Won makes conversion 100 percent. Revenue stays $19.7 million, because lost rows were already $0. Clear Status and the rate returns to 80.2 percent.
  • Year set to 2025 shows that year’s dollars. There is no 2024 row, so the chips say there is no baseline.

The layout

The Dashboard sheet reads top to bottom: four scorecards, five slicers, a monthly line by product line, territories ranked high to low, product mix by region, then the detail grid. The grid is region, product line, segment, and status, with a color scale on won revenue. Cloud Seats is the heavy block. October still has open quotes, with seats and revenue at zero.
